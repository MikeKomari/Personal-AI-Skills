# Financial Analysis Skill

## Objective

Evaluate an investment as a combination of **business quality, financial performance, valuation, catalysts, downside risk, and expected return**.

A good company is not automatically a good investment. Always separate company quality from price attractiveness.

## Required workflow

### 1. Define the security

Identify:
- ticker and exchange
- company
- sector/industry
- current price and currency
- market capitalization when relevant
- analysis date
- user's intended horizon if known

For current prices, financial results, valuation multiples, analyst estimates, or recent events, use fresh sources.

### 2. Understand the business

Explain:
- what the company sells;
- who pays;
- how it makes money;
- major segments;
- geographic exposure;
- competitive advantages;
- key dependencies;
- major competitors.

### 3. Analyze financial quality

Review when available:
- revenue growth
- gross margin
- operating margin
- net margin
- free cash flow
- ROIC/ROE
- operating leverage
- debt and liquidity
- share dilution/buybacks
- cash conversion
- earnings quality

Prefer company filings and official financial reports.

### 4. Analyze growth

Determine:
- historical growth;
- likely growth drivers;
- TAM/market growth where relevant;
- pricing power;
- volume growth;
- margin expansion potential;
- reinvestment opportunities.

Distinguish management guidance from independent expectations.

### 5. Valuation

Use methods appropriate to the company:
- P/E
- EV/EBITDA
- EV/Sales
- P/S
- FCF yield
- DCF
- dividend-based valuation
- sum-of-the-parts
- comparable companies

Do not force a DCF when assumptions are too uncertain.

State assumptions explicitly:
- revenue growth
- margins
- tax
- capex
- working capital
- WACC/discount rate
- terminal growth
- multiple assumptions

Use scenarios where useful:
- bear
- base
- bull

### 6. Catalysts

Identify realistic catalysts:
- earnings
- product launches
- rate changes
- regulation
- acquisitions
- capacity expansion
- commodity prices
- corporate actions
- industry cycles

Never invent catalysts.

### 7. Risks

Cover:
- valuation risk
- business risk
- balance-sheet risk
- regulatory risk
- competitive risk
- macro risk
- currency risk
- concentration risk
- thesis-specific risks

### 8. Investment conclusion

Classify the opportunity as one of:

- **Strong opportunity**
- **Attractive**
- **Watch / fairly valued**
- **Unattractive**
- **Avoid**

Do not use these labels merely because a stock recently rose or fell.

Explain:
- what must be true for the thesis to work;
- what price/valuation would make it attractive;
- what would invalidate the thesis.

## Required distinction

Always separate:

**Business quality:** How good is the company?

**Valuation:** How much am I paying?

**Expected return:** What could I reasonably make from this price?

**Risk:** What could permanently impair capital?

## Output

1. Executive view
2. Business
3. Financials
4. Growth
5. Competitive position
6. Valuation
7. Catalysts
8. Risks
9. Scenario analysis
10. Investment conclusion
11. Key metrics to monitor
12. Sources

## Guardrails

- Current prices must be sourced.
- Do not present analyst targets as facts.
- Do not provide false precision.
- Do not confuse revenue growth with value creation.
- Do not ignore dilution or debt.
- For Indonesian stocks, consider IDX context, BI rates, IDR, commodities, and local regulation where relevant.
- For US stocks, consider Fed policy, Treasury yields, USD, sector valuation, and US regulation where relevant.
- If data is missing, say what is missing instead of guessing.
