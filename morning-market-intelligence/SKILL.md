# Morning World & Market Intelligence Skill

## Mission

Every morning, produce a **fresh, evidence-backed, decision-oriented briefing**.

The goal is not to repeat headlines. The goal is to answer:

1. **What happened in the world?**
2. **Why does it matter economically and financially?**
3. **How could it affect crypto, US markets, and Indonesia?**
4. **What should I watch today?**
5. **Which US and Indonesian stocks currently have attractive risk/reward?**

This skill requires current web research whenever current events, prices, market data, or recent developments are discussed.

---

# PART 1 — WORLD → MARKETS

## Step 1: Establish the morning cutoff

Determine the current date and time and define the relevant overnight/recent-news window.

Prioritize developments since the previous US market close and major developments from the preceding 24 hours.

Do not present old news as today's news.

## Step 2: Scan the world

Search across these categories:

### Geopolitics
- wars/conflicts
- US-China relations
- sanctions
- elections
- diplomatic developments
- major political decisions

### Macro
- Fed
- ECB
- Bank of Japan
- Bank Indonesia
- inflation
- employment
- GDP
- interest rates
- bond yields
- fiscal policy

### Commodities
- oil
- natural gas
- gold
- industrial metals
- major agricultural commodities when relevant

### Technology
- AI
- semiconductors
- major technology regulation
- major product/industry developments

### Corporates

- guidance changes

- major corporate actions

### Indonesia
- government policy
- BI
- inflation
- GDP
- trade
- rupiah
- commodities
- major listed companies
- banking
- regulation
- foreign investment flows

Do not force every category into the report. Include only material developments.

---

# Step 3: Rank news by market importance

Rank events using:

**Market impact × probability of persistence × breadth of affected assets × surprise**

Use three labels:

- 🔴 **High impact**
- 🟡 **Medium impact**
- 🟢 **Low impact**

Do not equate media attention with market importance.

---

# Step 4: Build the transmission mechanism

For every major event, explain:

**Event → Economic mechanism → Market variable → Asset impact**

Example:

> Hawkish Fed signal  
> → higher expected policy rates  
> → higher Treasury yields / tighter financial conditions  
> → stronger USD and higher discount rates  
> → potential pressure on long-duration equities and crypto

Never jump directly from headline to price prediction.

Explicitly distinguish:

- direct effects;
- indirect effects;
- second-order effects;
- uncertain effects.

# PART 2 — INVESTMENT OPPORTUNITIES

## Step 7: Generate stock candidates

Identify potentially attractive:

### US stocks
Aim for 3–5 candidates.

### Indonesian stocks
Aim for 3–5 candidates.

Do not force recommendations. If no stock has an attractive risk/reward profile, say so.

Candidates can come from:
- quality businesses;
- temporary mispricing;
- improving fundamentals;
- attractive valuation;
- structural growth;
- macro beneficiaries;
- event-driven situations.

---

# Step 8: Analyze each candidate

For every candidate provide:

| Item | Required analysis |
|---|---|
| Thesis | Why the stock may outperform |
| Business quality | Competitive position and economics |
| Financials | Growth, margins, cash flow, balance sheet |
| Valuation | Current valuation vs history/peers/intrinsic value |
| Catalyst | What could cause repricing |
| Risk | What could make the thesis wrong |
| Macro sensitivity | Relevant rates, FX, commodities, etc. |
| Time horizon | Short / medium / long term |
| Conviction | Low / medium / high |
| Action | Buy / watch / avoid |

Current prices and valuation inputs must be fresh.

---

# Step 9: Valuation discipline

Never recommend:

> "Buy because the company is good."

Instead answer:

> "Is the expected return attractive at today's price?"

Use appropriate metrics:
- P/E
- EV/EBITDA
- P/S
- FCF yield
- DCF
- dividend yield
- price/book for appropriate financial businesses
- comparable-company valuation

Use scenario analysis when useful.

---

# Step 10: Separate recommendation types

Classify ideas as:

### Core
High-quality business suitable for longer holding periods.

### Opportunistic
Attractive because of temporary valuation or event.

### Speculative
High uncertainty/high upside; clearly label it.

Never mix speculative ideas with core investments without warning.

---

# Step 11: Portfolio awareness

If the user's existing holdings are known in the current context, consider concentration and correlation.

For example, do not repeatedly recommend another highly correlated technology stock without explaining concentration risk.

If holdings are not known in the current conversation, do not pretend to know them.

# SOURCE HIERARCHY

Prefer:

1. Central banks and governments
2. SEC/company filings and official company releases
3. Official statistical agencies
4. IMF / World Bank / BIS / OECD
5. Major exchanges and index providers
6. High-quality financial journalism
7. Reputable research institutions
8. Other reputable sources

Use multiple sources for major claims.

Do not rely on a single news article for a consequential investment conclusion.

---

# FACT / ANALYSIS / FORECAST DISCIPLINE

Clearly distinguish:

**FACT**
What happened and is supported by evidence.

**ANALYSIS**
What the evidence implies through an economic/financial mechanism.

**FORECAST**
What may happen next.

Never write a forecast as if it were a fact.

---

# INVESTMENT SAFETY RULES

This is analytical research, not guaranteed financial advice.

Never:
- guarantee returns;
- claim certainty;
- fabricate financial data;
- invent catalysts;
- invent prices;
- use stale prices as current prices;
- blindly follow analyst ratings;
- recommend a stock solely because it is trending;
- ignore valuation;
- hide downside risk;
- confuse correlation with causation.

When evidence is weak, say:

> **Insufficient evidence for a high-conviction recommendation.**

That is a valid outcome.
