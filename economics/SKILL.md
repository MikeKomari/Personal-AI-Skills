# Economics Skill

## Objective

Explain economic outcomes through **models, incentives, constraints, institutions, and evidence**, rather than generic business commentary.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the economic problem

Identify:
- actors;
- incentives;
- scarce resources;
- constraints;
- market/institution;
- relevant time horizon;
- geography;
- outcome being explained.

### 2. Choose the model

Use the simplest useful model:
- supply and demand
- elasticity
- consumer/producer surplus
- externalities
- asymmetric information
- principal-agent problems
- game theory
- market structure
- comparative advantage
- monetary transmission
- fiscal multiplier
- Phillips curve
- IS-LM / AD-AS where useful
- exchange-rate mechanisms
- capital flows
- business cycles
- growth models

Do not use jargon when a simple causal explanation is sufficient.

### 3. Explain the mechanism

Use:

**Shock → Incentive/constraint → Behavior → Market adjustment → Second-order effects**

Explicitly identify short-run versus long-run effects.

### 4. Consider competing mechanisms

Ask:
- What else could explain the outcome?
- What assumptions does the model require?
- Which actors may respond strategically?
- What feedback loops exist?
- What could reverse the initial effect?

### 5. Apply evidence

For current macroeconomic analysis, use current:
- inflation
- employment
- GDP
- interest rates
- central-bank statements
- exchange rates
- commodities
- trade data
- fiscal data
- capital flows

Prefer central banks, statistical agencies, governments, IMF, World Bank, BIS, OECD, and high-quality research.

### 6. Analyze policy

For a policy question:
- identify the policy objective;
- mechanism;
- beneficiaries;
- losers;
- short-term effects;
- long-term effects;
- unintended consequences;
- distributional effects;
- implementation constraints.

### 7. Conclusion

State:
- base case;
- strongest supporting mechanism;
- major uncertainty;
- alternative scenario;
- indicators that would confirm or reject the thesis.

## Special rule for markets

Never say:

> "X happened, therefore the stock/crypto price will rise."

Instead explain the transmission mechanism:

**Economic event → expected cash flows / discount rate / liquidity / risk premium → asset pricing effect**

Then state uncertainty.

## Output

1. Economic question
2. Relevant model
3. Mechanism
4. Evidence
5. Second-order effects
6. Alternative scenarios
7. Implications
8. Conclusion
9. Sources

## Indonesia lens

When relevant, explicitly consider:
- Bank Indonesia
- BI rate
- inflation
- IDR
- fiscal policy
- trade balance
- commodities
- foreign capital flows
- household consumption
- banking system
- China/US exposure

Always distinguish Indonesian-specific effects from global effects.
