# Research Skill

## Objective

Produce rigorous, decision-useful research rather than a collection of summaries. Start by defining the question, identify what must be established, gather high-quality evidence, evaluate source quality, reconcile disagreement, and state conclusions with calibrated confidence.

## Core workflow

1. **Frame the research question**
   - Restate the exact question.
   - Identify scope, geography, time period, and definitions.
   - Break the question into 3–7 answerable subquestions.
   - Identify what evidence would change the conclusion.

2. **Build a source plan**
   Prefer, in order when appropriate:
   - Primary sources: official documents, filings, datasets, laws, papers, company releases, government statistics.
   - High-quality secondary sources: reputable journalism, established research institutions, respected industry publications.
   - Tertiary sources only for discovery or orientation.
   - Never treat search snippets as evidence.

3. **Search iteratively**
   - Search each major subquestion separately.
   - Look for both supporting and contradicting evidence.
   - Search for recent developments when the topic is time-sensitive.
   - For academic work, prioritize peer-reviewed papers and authoritative repositories.
   - For company claims, distinguish company statements from independently verified facts.

4. **Evaluate evidence**
   For important claims, assess:
   - source authority
   - recency
   - methodology
   - incentives/bias
   - sample/data quality
   - whether the source actually supports the claim
   - whether newer evidence supersedes it

5. **Synthesize**
   Organize findings around the user's question, not around the order sources were found.
   Separate:
   - **Fact:** directly supported by evidence.
   - **Inference:** reasonable conclusion from multiple facts.
   - **Hypothesis/speculation:** plausible but insufficiently established.

6. **Resolve conflicts**
   When credible sources disagree:
   - state the disagreement;
   - explain why they differ;
   - compare methodology/data/date;
   - do not manufacture consensus.

7. **Conclude**
   Give:
   - direct answer;
   - strongest evidence;
   - important caveats;
   - confidence level;
   - what new evidence could change the conclusion.

## Citation rules

- Cite factual claims that materially depend on external research.
- Put citations immediately after the supported claim.
- Prefer direct/primary sources for critical claims.
- Include publication dates for time-sensitive sources.
- Never fabricate citations, quotations, statistics, studies, or source details.
- Never imply that a source says something it does not say.

## Output structure

Use this structure unless the user requests another:

1. **Executive answer**
2. **What we know**
3. **Evidence**
4. **Conflicting evidence / uncertainty**
5. **Analysis**
6. **Conclusion**
7. **Sources**

For long research, add a concise methodology section.

## Quality bar

Do not optimize for the number of sources. Optimize for:
**relevance × authority × freshness × methodological quality × triangulation.**

If evidence is insufficient, explicitly say so.
