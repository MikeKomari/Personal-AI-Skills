# Product Management Skill

## Objective

Determine **what should be built, for whom, why, and how to validate it** before optimizing implementation.

## Workflow

### 1. Problem

Define:
- target user;
- context;
- job-to-be-done;
- current workaround;
- pain;
- frequency;
- severity;
- willingness to pay/change behavior.

Do not accept a proposed solution as proof that a problem exists.

### 2. User segmentation

Identify:
- primary user;
- buyer;
- beneficiary;
- supplier/partner;
- decision maker;
- early adopter.

For marketplaces, distinguish all sides explicitly.

### 3. Value proposition

State:

**For [user], who has [problem], the product provides [outcome] because [reason to believe].**

Compare against existing alternatives, including doing nothing.

### 4. Market

Analyze:
- TAM/SAM/SOM when useful;
- market growth;
- market structure;
- fragmentation;
- switching costs;
- regulation;
- distribution channels;
- network effects.

Avoid unsupported giant TAM numbers.

### 5. Competition

Compare:
- direct competitors;
- indirect alternatives;
- incumbents;
- substitutes;
- internal/manual processes.

Identify the product's actual wedge.

### 6. Business model

Analyze:
- who pays;
- pricing;
- revenue model;
- gross margin;
- acquisition cost;
- retention;
- repeat frequency;
- transaction size;
- take rate;
- operational costs;
- unit economics.

For marketplaces, consider:
- supply acquisition;
- demand acquisition;
- liquidity;
- matching rate;
- fill rate;
- take rate;
- disintermediation;
- trust/safety.

### 7. Validation

Design the cheapest experiment that can disprove the core assumption.

Prioritize:
- customer interviews;
- landing pages;
- fake-door tests;
- concierge MVP;
- manual matching;
- prototypes;
- preorders;
- pilot customers.

Avoid building a full product before validating the riskiest assumption.

### 8. Prioritization

Evaluate features using:
- user impact;
- business impact;
- confidence;
- effort;
- strategic importance.

Do not equate "requested" with "important."

### 9. Metrics

Define:
- North Star Metric;
- acquisition;
- activation;
- engagement;
- retention;
- conversion;
- revenue;
- referral;
- operational quality.

Metrics must correspond to actual value creation.

### 10. Recommendation

End with:
- build;
- validate first;
- pivot;
- defer;
- do not build.

State the evidence and assumptions behind the decision.

## Output

1. Problem
2. Users
3. Current alternatives
4. Value proposition
5. Market
6. Competition
7. Business model
8. Key assumptions
9. Validation experiments
10. MVP
11. Metrics
12. Risks
13. Recommendation

## Anti-patterns

- Feature-first thinking
- "Everyone is our customer"
- Vanity metrics
- Unsupported TAM
- Copying competitors without a wedge
- Building before validation
- Assuming users behave as stated
- Confusing engagement with value
